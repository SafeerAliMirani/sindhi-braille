You are checking the Sindhi of a book for blind and sighted children to read
together. It is an original book by Dr Safeer Ali Mirani, for Sindhi class 1,
for a child who cannot yet read. Every page is printed in ink and embossed in
Standard Sindhi Braille on the same sheet.

Check the Sindhi and return the whole text back to me corrected, in exactly the
same shape, changing nothing else. Then, after the text, list what you changed
and why, briefly.

WHAT I NEED YOU TO CHECK

1. Is every sentence natural Sindhi that a Sindhi adult would actually say?
   Some of it was drafted by someone who is not a native speaker. Two I already
   doubt: ڪٽ as the heading for subtraction, and مدد ڪرڻ سٺي ڳالھ آهي.

2. Is every word right for a six-year-old who is meeting it for the first time?
   Replace anything too abstract, keeping the same letters where you can.

2a. IMPORTANT, from the last round. Do not shorten a line by deleting a word
   the grammar needs. ٿو، ٿي، ٿا، آهي and postpositions like تي stay. If a line
   is too long, say so and suggest a shorter sentence that is still whole,
   rather than removing the auxiliary. And do not remove a sentence: the line
   about حضرت محمد ﷺ and the line naming the colours of the flag are wanted.

3. Spelling, and one rule in particular. ه (U+0647) and ھ (U+06BE) are two
   different letters with two different braille cells: ه is dots 1-2-5 and ھ is
   dots 2-3-6. ھ belongs ONLY to the aspirate digraphs جھ گھ ڙھ لھ مھ نھ ڻھ, so
   گھر، پڙھو، ڳالھ، جھنڊو، ڳاڙھو take ھ, and هڪ، آهي، هن، نه، ته take ه. Getting
   this wrong embosses the wrong cell. Mark any word where you think I have
   chosen wrongly.

4. THIS IS THE PRINT SPELLING, so keep it correct Sindhi. The braille is made
   from this same text by software, and the software already writes ء wherever
   it meets ئ or ؤ, because the code has one hamza. So صفائي and سائو and ڳئون
   stay as they are: the page prints them correctly and the braille handles
   itself. Do NOT respell them as صفاءي، ساءو، ڳانء - that corrupts the printed
   book to fix something that was never broken. (I asked for this the wrong way
   round last time and you did as you were told.)

   Do avoid these, which the software rewrites in a way that changes the word:
   أ إ ٱ ة ہ ی ك. Write ا for أ إ ٱ, ه for ة and ہ, ي for ی, ڪ for ك.

5. No vowel diacritics anywhere: no زبر، زير، پيش، شد، جزم.

6. KEEP EVERY LINE SHORT. A line must fit 22 braille cells, which is roughly 22
   Sindhi letters counting spaces. Lines longer than that break across two lines
   of braille and a beginner loses the sentence. Shorten anything longer.

WHAT NOT TO CHANGE

Leave every line beginning with @ exactly as it is. @heading is a lesson
heading, @table is a chart the software fills in, @shape is a raised drawing.
Do not renumber the lessons. Do not add or remove lessons. Keep one thought per
line.
