STYLE
Age six, first year of school. Short sentences. Concrete nouns a child can

the next line. Warm and plain. No moral lectures.

HARD CONSTRAINTS
These are not style preferences. The text is converted to braille by software,
and breaking these makes the printed page and the braille page disagree.

1. DO NOT USE THESE CHARACTERS AT ALL:
      ئ  ؤ  أ  إ  ٱ  ة  ہ  ی  ك
   The braille converter rewrites each of them, so a word containing one would
   be spelled differently in braille than in print. Use the standard Sindhi
   equivalents instead:
      ئ and ؤ  ->  ء
      أ إ ٱ    ->  ا
      ة and ہ  ->  ه
      ی        ->  ي
      ك        ->  ڪ   (Sindhi kaf, not the Arabic one)

2. ه AND ھ ARE TWO DIFFERENT LETTERS WITH TWO DIFFERENT BRAILLE CELLS.
   ه (U+0647) is the ordinary he, braille 1-2-5: هي، هن، هو، هڪ، آهي، نه، ته.
   ھ (U+06BE) is do-chashmi and belongs ONLY to the aspirate digraphs
   جھ، گھ، ڙھ، لھ، مھ، نھ، ڻھ — for example گھر، پڙھ، ڳالھيون، سمھ.
   Write ه wherever an ordinary he is meant. Do not substitute one for the
   other: a word with the wrong one embosses the wrong cell.

3. DO NOT PUT VOWEL DIACRITICS on words in the lesson text. No زبر, no زير,
   no پيش, no شد, no jazam. Plain letters only. Diacritics are taught in a
   separate part of the book.

4. KEEP EACH SENTENCE UNDER ABOUT 40 CHARACTERS so it fits one braille line.

5. PUNCTUATION: only the full stop (.) and the comma (،) and the question mark
   (؟). No quotation marks, no brackets, no dashes, no colons, no semicolons,
   no exclamation marks.
