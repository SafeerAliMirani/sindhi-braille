# The lessons — what I got wrong, and what to ask Gemini for

## 1. The honest answer to your question

**I added no lessons.** I built letters, words and one sentence a page, and
called it a book. That is a قاعدو, a drill book. You asked for the thing the
real book has: **سبق** — numbered lessons with a title and a piece to read.

You are right and my table of contents was the wrong shape.

## 3. The corrected shape for your book

The letters still have to be taught, but they stop being the book. They become
the first part of it, and then the lessons begin.

**Part 1 — الف ب.** The alphabet, all 52 letters with their cells, in the order
the corpus gives. Pages 5 to 30 as before. This part is drill and should be.

**Part 2 — بارکڙي.** The marks and the syllabary. Pages 31 to 34.

**Part 3 — سبق.** *The book.* Twelve to sixteen numbered lessons, each with a
title and a short piece to read. Pages 35 onward, about two pages each.

**Part 4 — شڪليون**, the shapes, embossed. Kept, because it is the one thing a
braille book can do that the printed book cannot do for a blind child.

## 4. The brief to paste into Gemini

Everything below the line is for Gemini. It is written so that what comes back
can go straight into the software.

---

**BRIEF — Sindhi text for a grade 1 braille primer**

I am producing a Sindhi reading book for grade 1, in Standard Sindhi Braille and
print together, for blind and sighted children to read side by side. I need the
Sindhi text written. Please follow these rules exactly.

**Write in Sindhi, in the Perso-Arabic script used in Pakistan.** Not Devanagari.

**For each lesson give me:**
1. a title, three words or fewer
2. a reading piece of 4 to 8 short sentences
3. six new words from that piece, as a word list
4. two simple questions about the piece

**Style.** Grade 1, age six. Short sentences. Concrete nouns a child can touch or
see. Repetition is good, not bad — a beginner should be able to predict the next
line. This is how a primer works.

**Hard constraints, because the text becomes braille:**

- **Do not use these characters at all: ئ ؤ أ إ ٱ ة ہ ی ك.** They have standard
  Sindhi equivalents and the braille translator rewrites them, so the printed and
  braille pages would disagree. Use ء ا ه ي ڪ instead.
- **Do not put vowel diacritics (زبر، زير، پيش) on words in the lesson text.**
  They are taught separately. Plain letters only in the pieces.
- **Keep sentences under about 40 characters** so a line fits the page.
- **Use the ordinary Sindhi full stop ۔ or . and comma ، only.** No quotation
  marks, no brackets, no dashes.

**The lessons.** [paste the fourteen titles from the table above]

**Also please check this word list** for spelling and for whether each word suits
a six-year-old, and replace any that do not:

هي · هن · ان · کير · وار · جو · هو · لال · تارو · بال · بيل · دل · مان · سان ·
مالا · ڪتاب · پير · شير · ڪن · گل · آهي · ڏند · ٿورو · پاڻي · ڌرتي · ڪڻڪ ·
قلم · ٻلي · ٻار · خط · فون · ٽوپي · صبح · حال · ٽماٽو · ڳوٺ · چار · زمين ·

## 5. What happens when it comes back

Every sentence goes through the checker before it goes in the book:

- does it use only letters the book has taught by that page?
- does it survive Sindhi → braille → Sindhi unchanged?

Anything that fails comes back to you with the reason. Three of my own fifty-two
words failed that test, so expect some of Gemini's to fail too. **That is the
check doing its job, not a problem.**

Then Riaz sees it, and he is the one who decides whether a six-year-old in Sindh
would actually want to read it.
