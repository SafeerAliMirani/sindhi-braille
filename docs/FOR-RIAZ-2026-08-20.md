## 1. Is it 100%? No, and here is every number

Nobody should say 100% about this, and the software does not say it. It reports
each thing it can measure separately, because they measure different things.
Here is the whole board, as it stands today.

| what is measured | result | what it does not tell you |
|---|---|---|
| worked examples printed in the guide | **68 of 68**, cell for cell **and space for space** | only that we read those pages right |
| a printed example we do **not** reproduce | **1** — the guide's addition, see below | it is recorded, not hidden |
| rules the guide states but never works an example of | 10 of 10 implemented as read | **nothing about whether our reading is right** |
| Sindhi words taken to braille and back | **99.94%** of 23,432 | that the software agrees with itself |
| words that do not survive | 4 spellings: عيسوي، تعالي، صلعم، ڻھ | each has a known reason |
| Grade 2 contractions, both directions | 238 of 243 | the 5 failures are cells the book gives to two words |
| letters against Arabic, Persian and Urdu braille | 94 of 99 agree | the 5 differences are letters those codes lack |
| the two engines against each other | 70 of 70 | they were written by one person |
| a 36-page document, browser against command line | identical | — |
| the liblouis tables | compile clean, 99 tests, 0 failures | that a screen reader *could* use them |

**So the honest sentence is this.** Where the code is written down and worked
through, the software reproduces it exactly, one printed example excepted and
that one recorded. Where the code is silent or contradicts itself, no test can
help, and only he can. And every figure above is the software checking itself or
checking a book. **One page read by touch, on 15 August, is still the only
evidence from outside.** That is exactly why the bigger test matters.

### What changed since the August reading

- **Arithmetic spacing.** He said a sign takes a space before it and none after.
  We had recorded that as an open question because we believed the guide
  disagreed. **We had misread the guide. He was right and the book agrees with
  him**: every one of the six equals signs it prints is closed up to the number
  after it. Both engines now do this.
- **What is still open on arithmetic, and it is narrow.** The guide closes `+` up
  to the 9 but spaces `−`, `×` and `÷`. One against three, with itself. We follow
  the three. Sheet 4 now asks him this, and only this.
- **الله** carries its shadda and khari zabar, with the shadda between the two
  lams, as he instructed.
- **The verse opening pair** stands on its own with a space after, as he
  corrected.
- **۽** now reads back correctly. It used to come back as اءين.
- **The page width** is now chosen before download, so words are not split
  across lines any more. That was the fault on the August page, and it was never
  the code.

## 2. The bigger test

The first test was one page of prose. It passed completely, and that is worth
something, but it carried no verse, no arithmetic, no contractions and no
numbers. A bigger test should be built so that **a failure can be located**, not
just noticed.

### What it should contain

Ten pages, in this order, each page one kind of material:

1. **Running prose**, three pages. The main thing. Fresh text he has not seen.
2. **Numbers and dates**, one page. Including the new arithmetic spacing.
3. **Verse**, one page. Opening marks, line ends, a pen-name.
4. **Grade 2 contractions**, one page, marked as such so he knows to expect them.
5. **Foreign words and proper nouns**, one page.
6. **The hard cells in context**, two pages. The nine cells that carry three or
   more meanings, each used in a real sentence, so the positional rules are
   actually exercised rather than assumed.
7. **A repeat of page 1**, at the end, unannounced. If page 1 and page 10 do not
   get the same result, we have learned something about fatigue rather than
   about the code, which is worth knowing before anyone signs anything.

### How to run it

- Every line numbered in print on the answer key, so he can say **"line 47"**
  and we can find it in one second.
- **Read cold.** He is not told what any line says.
- Someone sighted marks each line: **right / wrong / hesitated**. Hesitation is
  data. A line read correctly but slowly is telling you something about the code.
- The score is a fraction, per page and overall. That is the number to put in
  front of the Education Department.

### What I need from you

**The Sindhi text.** I will not write the prose: making up Sindhi sentences is
how you get a test that passes because it is easy. Send me fresh material he has
not seen — news, a story, a school text — and I will lay out the ten pages, the
answer key and the scoring sheet, all numbered.

### On the approval

Do not take the first test to the Department. Take this one, with a score, a
scoring sheet in someone's handwriting, and the date. "It was read correctly" is
an opinion. "212 of 214 lines, read cold, by the President of PAB Sindh, on this
date" is a result.

## 3. The paper

**Good news first: 150 gsm is the right weight.** Braille paper is 120 to 160
gsm, so the chart paper the katib press sells sits in the middle of the range and
the dots will hold. This is not a compromise.

**The size matters more than he may expect**, because braille geometry does not
scale. Every cell is 6.2 mm wide and every line 10 mm apart, always. So the width
of the paper decides the number of cells, and nothing else does.

Run `python tools/paper_plan.py` for the full table. The important row:

| cut from the chart sheet | size | with a 12 mm binding margin |
|---|---|---|
| **quarter of a 22 x 28 in sheet** | 279 x 356 mm | **40 cells x 33 lines** |
| A4 | 210 x 297 mm | 28 cells x 27 lines |

**40 cells is the international braille page width.** Cutting the chart sheet
into quarters gives a standard braille page on paper he can buy in Larkana.
Cutting it to A4 throws away twelve cells on every line and a third of the page.

So the question to put to the katib press is not "can you cut A4" but **"what is
the largest clean rectangle you can cut from your sheet, and what exactly is
your sheet's size in inches?"** Send me that and I will give the exact cells and
lines, and build the test at that width.

**On the binding margin.** Braille is read left to right in every language,
Sindhi included, so the binding margin goes on the **left** and comes off before
the cells. 12 mm is enough for a punch and a comb. It is already in the table
above; nothing needs adding to it afterwards.

**One warning.** A `.brf` file has no page size in it. It is written at a fixed
number of cells per line, and if that number is larger than the paper, the
machine breaks the lines itself, in the middle of words. That is what happened in
August. Once he tells us the cut size, every file for this test gets built at
that width and no other.
