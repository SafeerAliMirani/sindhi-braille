# The book, redesigned: an original reader for one to two years

**Written by Dr Safeer Ali Mirani. Nothing taken from the Sindh Textbook Board
book, or from any other book. Ideas from wherever they are good; text entirely
new.**

---

## 1. What changes when the book has to last two years

A book for one grade is a primer. A book a child grows with for two years is a
**course**, and it needs a different spine: a clear road from a child who has
never touched a letter to one who reads without help.

That means starting earlier than any converted textbook starts.

---

## 2. The part only this book can have

**A sighted child arrives at reading with a trained eye.** It has been tracking,
comparing shapes and following lines since infancy. **A blind child arrives with
a finger that has done none of that** — and a converted textbook offers nothing
for it, because the book it was converted from had no reason to.

So the book opens with six pages that contain **no Sindhi at all**:

| page | what it trains |
|---|---|
| 1 | one dot, two dots, three, a full cell — is this row more than the last |
| 2 | blocks and gaps — where does something begin and end |
| 3 | **a line the width of the page** — run a finger across without lifting it |
| 4 | **finding the start of the next line** — where beginners actually get lost |
| 5 | same or different, one pair at a time — the whole skill, before meaning |
| 6 | counting in fives — so the hand moves in units, which is what makes reading fast |

**Built already. `test-sheets/print-6-readiness.brf`.** Emboss it and give it to
Riaz, and ask him whether the order is right — he has taught blind children and I
have not. Page 4 in particular may deserve more pages than one.

This is the strongest argument for writing the book rather than converting one.
It is also the part a child may spend weeks on, and those weeks are saved later,
not lost.

## 3. Two volumes, not one

A hundred-page book, embossed single-sided, is a hundred sheets hand-fed through
your printer twice. That is not a gift, it is an ordeal.

**Volume One — the year of learning to read.** About 56 pages.

| part | pages | what |
|---|---|---|
| readiness | 6 | the finger, before any letter |
| the letters | 26 | 13 lessons, all 52 letters, in corpus order |
| بارکڙي | 6 | the marks and the syllabary |
| first words and sentences | 10 | reading, in the smallest possible pieces |
| numbers | 4 | the number sign and the digits |
| front and back matter | 4 | title, a note to the teacher, the alphabet chart |

**Volume Two — the year of reading.** About 60 pages.

| part | pages | what |
|---|---|---|
| the lessons | 40 | 20 numbered سبق, two pages each |
| shapes by touch | 6 | square, triangle, circle, rectangle, and what they are for |
| longer reading | 8 | three or four pieces of real length |
| the marks that confuse | 4 | including the cell that never fully resolves |
| back matter | 2 | |

**Finish and give Volume One.** It is a complete thing on its own: a child who
works through it can read. Volume Two follows when it follows.

## 4. On originality, and how to use other books

**Ideas are free; text is not.** Take the *shape* of a lesson from anywhere —
Sindhi readers, English beginning readers, whatever is good — and write the
Sindhi yourself.

What is worth borrowing from good English beginning readers, because Sindhi
primers often do not do it:

- **A controlled vocabulary that grows on purpose**, so each piece is readable
  with what the child already has. We enforce this in software.
- **Repetition as structure, not padding.** A beginner should be able to predict
  the next line. That is what makes them feel able.
- **A story with a small arc**, even in six sentences. Something happens.
- **Questions that ask what the child thinks**, not only what the text said.

What is worth borrowing from the Sindhi tradition:

- **Numbered سبق with titles.** A child knows where they are in the book.
- **سرگرمي, activities** after a lesson.
- **A poem**, because rhythm is the oldest reading aid there is and it works
  better under a finger than most things.

**In the colophon, say what influenced it.** Not a citation list; a sentence of
honest acknowledgement. It costs nothing and it is the difference between
original work and work that looks nervous.

---

## 5. "Advanced content for children who cannot yet read"

That is the right ambition and it is not a contradiction. It means: **simple
words, real ideas.**

A six-year-old cannot read the word evaporation. A six-year-old can absolutely
understand that water goes up into the sky and comes back down, and can read a
piece that says so in eight short sentences. The limit is the vocabulary, not the
thinking.

So the lessons should carry actual content: where water comes from, why we help
each other, what a triangle is, why the red light means stop. **Never talk down.**
A child who is being taught to read by touch has already worked harder than most
adults reading this.
