# The first reading — 15 August 2026

**Riaz Hussain Memon read a page of Standard Sindhi Braille produced by this
software, by touch, from embossed paper, and read it complete and without a
single error.**

That sentence is the reason the project exists. Everything else in this
repository is one implementation checking another, or a machine checking a book.
This is the only check that comes from outside.

---

## What was read

A passage of Sindhi news prose he had not seen before — a report on presidential
approval of civil and military awards, six sentences, running to proper nouns,
digits, foreign names and a comma-heavy structure. It was typed into the
translator as ordinary Sindhi, embossed, and handed to him.

He read it through. No corrections, no hesitation reported, nothing skipped.

## What went wrong, and what it was not

**Words were split across lines.** This was not the braille and not the code. The
site's download was writing **40 cells per line**, the international braille-paper
width. A4 fits **28**. The embosser received lines longer than the paper and broke
them itself, and a machine breaking a line has no idea where a word ends.

Fixed by putting the width in the writer's hands: the site now has a *cells per
line* control that defaults to **28 · A4**, the file is named for its width, and
the browser's line-breaker is a port of the Python one, so it breaks at word
boundaries and hyphenates only a word genuinely wider than the page.

**The lesson is worth keeping:** a `.brf` has to be written at the width of the
machine that will emboss it. There is no such thing as a width-neutral braille
file.

## What he found in the code

Six things. Two were settled on the spot, two are implemented, two are open.

### Settled

**ک in a borrowed Urdu word.** Sindhi ک and Urdu ک are one Unicode character,
worth two cells in Sindhi and one in Urdu. He ruled that it needs no special
handling: Sindhi shares many letters with Urdu and a reader reads that letter the
same way whichever language the word came from. **One script, one value per
letter; the software never branches on the language of a word.**

**وزيراعظم read as one word.** It was one word in the source text. Not a fault in
the translator.

### Implemented

**الله must carry its marks.** Ordinary Sindhi writes it bare; the shadda and the
khari zabar belong to it. His rule — the shadda falls *between* the two lams —
turns out to be the Arabic braille convention: liblouis's own Arabic table states
that the shadda is written before the letter it doubles, and the Urdu table gives
the whole ligature as **1-6-123-4-125**, putting the khari zabar after the lam and
before the ه. Every one of those cells is the same cell in Sindhi. It is now
written **ا ل shadd ل khari-zabar ه**, keeping both lams as he described.

**The opening verse marks stand alone.** The two 12356 that head a stanza were
being joined to the first word. They are their own group now, with a space after.
The guide states the pair, and states that the *closing* mark is attached; it
never says whether the opening pair is. He settled it.

### Open, and on sheet 4

**The spacing of arithmetic.** He said `8 +9 =17` and that the international rules
have changed. They have, and they support him in substance but not in detail. UEB
— the current international code — makes a distinction this code does not:
**operation signs are unspaced, comparison signs are spaced**, so its own worked
example of `8 + 9 = 17` is embossed `8+9 = 17`. The standard guide is on the other
side entirely: five worked sums on pp.49–50, every sign spaced on both sides, and
those five are part of the 69 printed examples this software reproduces exactly.

Nothing has been changed. `test-sheets/print-4-decisions.brf` carries the same sum
in all three spacings, labelled الف. ب. ج., for him to read cold.

**His point about the full stop is true of UEB and not of Sindhi.** In UEB the
decimal point and the full stop are the same cell, which is exactly why a full
stop cannot follow a number there. In this code they differ — the guide gives the
decimal point as **dot 2** (p.50) and the full stop as **2-5-6**. The clash he is
warning about does not exist here, and copying UEB would create it.

**The end of a line of verse.** "Punctuation if there is any, otherwise a space"
is his phrase and it needs to be his words before it goes into two engines.

## The stray ا

He noticed an extra ا near لطيف. It was **۽**, and two faults were stacked under
it.

The sign is written out in full as اَئين, so read back letter by letter it came
out **اءين** rather than ۽. And the word list itself carried **مين (747)** and
**اءين (488)** as entries — meaning the corpus it was built from had both signs
expanded before anyone counted them, at exactly the frequencies you would expect
of *in* and *and*. Two more entries were the same debris, ۽ run into the following
word.

Both are fixed. ۾ and ۽ round-trip exactly now, and the built-in self test went
from 12 exact to 14.

## The braille writer

He tried the six-key chord entry on the site and it did not behave. The chord was
tracked with a counter — one up per key pressed, one down per key released, write
the cell at zero. But the operating system *repeats* keydown while a key is held,
so holding a chord pushed the counter up with no matching release and it never
came back to zero. Someone feeling for the keys holds them; someone testing taps
them. Replaced with the set of keys actually down.

---

## What this does and does not establish

It establishes that the code, as this software writes it, is readable as Sindhi by
a Sindhi braille reader across ordinary running prose — which is the thing no
amount of self-consistency could establish.

It does not establish that every rule is right. It was **one reader and one page**,
and the passage was prose: it carried no verse, no arithmetic, no contractions.
Sheets 1 to 4 exist to reach the parts the news page did not.
