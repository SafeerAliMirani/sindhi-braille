# Lattice Script

**A design philosophy.**

There are systems that were complete before anyone thought to draw them. They sit
in the world already ordered, waiting for someone patient enough to notice the
order and careful enough not to disturb it. This movement is concerned only with
that noticing. It does not invent structure; it reveals structure that was always
there, and it does so with the restraint of someone who understands that the
subject is more interesting than any treatment of it.

**Space and form.** The grid is not a convenience here, it is the argument. Every
element occupies a position that was determined before the designer arrived, and
the composition earns its authority by refusing to improve on that arrangement.
Marks repeat. They repeat because repetition is what the system does, and the eye
that follows them across the field begins to feel the rule before it is told the
rule. Negative space is generous but never decorative: it is the silence between
rows that lets a reader hear the pattern. Nothing floats. Nothing is placed for
balance alone. Each object sits on a coordinate, and the coordinates were given.

**Colour and material.** Colour carries information or it does not appear. A
narrow band of muted pigments, closer to mineral than to ink, distinguishes one
family of forms from another and does nothing else. No gradient, no shadow, no
glow. The palette should feel as though it were mixed once, decades ago, for a
specific scientific purpose and never revised. Where colour is absent the paper
must be warm enough to feel like a surface rather than a void, because the work
is about things that will eventually be touched.

**Scale and rhythm.** The unit is small and it is honest. Where a form has a true
physical dimension in the world, that dimension is respected on the page rather
than enlarged for effect. Rhythm comes from the count itself: nine, then eleven,
then nine, then ten. A composition that lets an uneven series stay uneven is more
truthful than one that squares it up, and the small irregularity is what proves
the pattern was found rather than imposed.

**Composition and hierarchy.** One statement at the top, quiet and certain. Then
the field, which is the whole point. Then, at the foot, the smallest possible
apparatus of attribution, set at a size that assumes the reader has already spent
time with the field and does not need to be sold anything. Typography whispers
throughout: thin, generously letterspaced at small sizes, never competing with
the marks. Labels are clinical. Numbers are monospaced. The document should read
like a plate from a reference work whose discipline does not quite exist.

**Craft.** This must look like the product of many hundreds of hours. Every
alignment deliberate, every interval measured, every colour tested against the
one beside it. The finished sheet should reward a viewer who leans in after ten
minutes and finds that a relationship they had assumed was decorative is in fact
exact. That is the standard: master-level execution, painstaking attention, and
absolutely no gesture that exists to impress rather than to describe.
